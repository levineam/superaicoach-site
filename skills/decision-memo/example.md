# Example: decision memo

Everything in this example is made up.

## What was pasted

I run Tallow Ridge Cabinetry. It's me, Luis in the shop, and Ines doing the books part time. I do all the installs myself and we're booked 7 weeks out. For the last four months I've said no to maybe 2 jobs a month because I can't get there. The average job is about $4,200 and we keep about 40 percent after materials. A second installer would be around $62,000 a year all in, that's my accountant's number. We have about $38,000 in the bank and our bills run around $19,000 a month. Winter is slow. Last January and February we did half our normal volume. My brother-in-law says just hire, but January worries me. Luis said he'd try installs if I paid him more. I don't know what to do.

## The memo

**The decision.** Whether to hire a second full-time installer, or handle the backlog another way.

**Options.**
1. Do nothing.
2. Hire a second installer at about $62,000 a year.
3. Pay Luis more to take on installs.

**Cost and risk.**
- Do nothing: no new cost. You keep turning away about 2 jobs a month, which is about $8,400 in sales and $3,360 kept each month. Customers who won't wait 7 weeks go elsewhere.
- Hire: about $5,200 a month on top of your $19,000 in bills. Your $38,000 covers about 2 months of bills now and about 1.5 months with the hire. Each job keeps about $1,680, so the hire pays for itself at about 37 extra jobs a year, around 3 a month. You are turning away about 2. Last January and February ran at half volume, so a full-time installer could sit idle when cash is tightest.
- Pay Luis more: costs the raise, amount not given. The shop may fall behind while he installs.

**What would have to be true.**
- Do nothing: you are fine with a 7-week wait and with losing about 2 jobs a month.
- Hire: you can fill about 3 extra jobs a month all year, winter included, and your cash can carry the slow months.
- Luis: he installs well enough that customers are happy, and the shop stays on schedule without him.

**Recommendation.** Try Luis first. By your numbers the hire needs about 3 extra jobs a month and you are turning away about 2. Winter cut your volume in half last year, and the hire would shrink your cash cushion to about 1.5 months. Luis costs a raise and he is willing. If he handles it and you are still turning away 3 or more jobs a month, look at the hire again.

**Cheapest test.** For 8 weeks, have Luis install the jobs you would have declined, with a temporary raise agreed in writing first. Track the jobs added, any shop jobs that ran late, and what each job kept. Count it as a yes if he takes on at least 4 jobs and the shop stays on schedule.

Pay, payroll and tax details for a new hire or a change to Luis's role are worth a call with your accountant. This memo isn't legal or tax advice.

Missing: the cost of Luis's raise, how much shop work would slip while he installs, and whether "maybe 2 jobs a month" is a firm count.
